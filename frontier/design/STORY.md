# Sable River — story bible

Original story, characters and dialogue. Autumn 1899 into the winter of 1900, in the fictional Sable River country.

## Premise
Ruth Caddell rides in from the west with a dead man's watch and a list of four names. Eighteen months ago her
husband Tom, a Wells-and-Merriweather express messenger (fictional company), was shot in the door of a payroll car
outside Harlan's Siding. The company paid the widow forty dollars and closed the book. Ruth didn't.

The names lead to the Sable River country, where the Meridian & Western Land and Rail Syndicate is buying the
basin acre by acre ahead of a drought, with money that isn't entirely clean and "range detectives" who are the men
on Ruth's list. Ruth means to kill four men. Instead she keeps picking up strays — and finds herself responsible for
people, which is harder than revenge and costs more.

**Theme:** what you owe the dead versus what you owe the living. Revenge narrows a life to a point; the Outfit keeps
widening Ruth's back out again. Standing (honour) measures which she chooses, scene by scene.

**Tone:** dry, observant, funny in the way tired people are funny; violence is sudden and ugly; the land is
beautiful and indifferent. No speeches. People rarely say the true thing directly.

## Ruth Caddell (protagonist)
34. Grew up on a Missouri horse farm; at sixteen ran off with "Colonel Brady's Frontier Exhibition" and became
"Miss Ruthie, the Ozark Wonder" — trick shooting from horseback, glass balls thrown in the air. Married Tom
Caddell at 24; served as a deputy in a Kansas county for three years (one of a handful of women to do it), quit when
Tom took the express job. Lean, weathered, a scar through the left eyebrow. Keeps her late husband's silver watch
wound but never sets it. Quiet with strangers, sharp with friends, honest to a fault except about herself. Shoots
like she was born to it, which is the problem: it is the one thing that has always been easy.
- **Nerve** (gameplay): the show-ring trick of seeing everything at once — the world slows the way it did in the ring
  with eight glass balls in the air.
- Voice: low, unhurried, Ozark vowels flattened by years in Kansas. Understatement is her weapon.

## The Outfit (companions, recruited over the story; camp at Willow Bend, then moving)
- **Hap Lindqvist** — 50s, Swedish drover, gut-shot when the syndicate's men ran his crew off; Ruth patches him up in
  chapter 1. Huge, gentle, cooks, sings hymns badly. The Outfit's conscience. Fate depends on chapter 5.
- **Cornelius Abernathy, M.D.** — 40s, struck off in Philadelphia after a patient died while he was drunk on
  laudanum. Brilliant, cruel when sober, kind when he shouldn't be. Joins in chapter 3 after Ruth hauls him out of a
  Mesquite Wells cantina to set a child's leg. Arc: whether he lets himself be needed.
- **Delphine "Del" Arceneaux** — 30, New Orleans cardsharp who works the riverboats and rail towns; owes the
  syndicate's money man in Port Linden. Joins in chapter 2 after a rigged poker game Ruth un-rigs. Fast, funny,
  lies as easily as breathing; the Outfit's eyes on money. Arc: staying when running would be smarter.
- **Joseph Kehoe (Two Rivers)** — 38, Lakota-Irish, scout for the army until Wounded Knee, which he saw and does not
  discuss. Tracker, horse trader, reader of newspapers. Joins in chapter 4 at Coldwater. Arc: whether the fight is his.
- **Billy Pruitt** — 14, runaway from a Thornwood logging camp, horse-mad, attaches himself to Ruth uninvited in
  chapter 2. Reminds her of herself at sixteen, which she does not enjoy. Arc: Ruth's choices become his example.
- **Ingrid Halvorsen** — 47, widowed rancher (Halvorsen Ranch) holding out against the syndicate. Ally, not a member;
  becomes one if Standing is high by chapter 5.

## Antagonists
- **Eben Shale** — leader of the Shale brothers (Eben, Cutter, little Asa) and Tom's killer. Now "chief of range
  detectives" for the syndicate. Charming, sentimental, keeps a family Bible he reads aloud; sincerely believes he is
  building something. The last name on the list.
- **Cutter Shale** — mean, clever; runs the syndicate's water-rights intimidation. Chapter 3 target.
- **Asa Shale** — 19, the youngest, who held the horses at Harlan's Siding. Ruth can spare him (chapter 4 choice);
  if spared he resurfaces in chapter 6.
- **Lucius Pell** — the syndicate's man in Port Linden, a lawyer with soft hands and a hard ledger. Never draws a gun;
  ruins people with paper. The real enemy, and much harder to shoot.
- **Arliss Doane** — the fourth name; dead before Ruth arrives (Ruth finds his grave in chapter 1 — someone is
  cleaning up loose ends).

## Supporting cast
- **Sheriff Odell Mabry** (Bitter Spring) — tired, decent once, owes the syndicate for his re-election. Can be turned.
- **Augustus Fenn** — editor of the Port Linden *Lantern*; wants the syndicate story; prints what Ruth brings him.
- **Mother Concepción Ruiz** — runs the soup kitchen and clinic at the San Lazaro mission ruin.
- **Tobias Greer** — trading post owner, sells to everyone, informs for whoever pays.
- **Rosa and Mateo Ybarra** — Mesquite Wells family whose well the syndicate wants.
- **"Saint" Gideon Crane** — itinerant preacher with a revival tent who preaches against the railroad.

## Structure (six chapters + epilogue)
1. **Willow Bend** (Bitter Spring, river valley). Arrival; Hap; the camp; the sheriff; Arliss Doane's fresh grave;
   first contact with the Shales' men. Teaches riding, camp, hunting, gunfights, Nerve, Standing.
2. **Paper and Iron** (Port Linden, Thornwood). The syndicate's offices; Fenn's newspaper; Del's poker game; Billy;
   a bank job that isn't what it seems; Pell's first move (Ruth's bounty poster appears).
3. **Dry Season** (Corrigan Plains, Mesquite Wells, Halvorsen Ranch). Water war; cattle drive through the Breaks;
   Doc; Cutter Shale's death or arrest (player choice).
4. **Silver and Snow** (Coldwater, Kestrel Range). Mine strike; avalanche; Joseph; Asa's fate (spare/kill).
5. **The Meridian Line** (rail line). Robbing the syndicate payroll train the way Tom's was robbed — the mirror of the
   crime. The Outfit splits over it; Hap's fate; Standing locks the ending branch.
6. **Long Light** (everywhere). Pell's counterstroke on the Outfit; the last ride for Eben Shale; endings:
   - *High Standing:* Ruth brings Shale in alive and Pell down in print; the Outfit scatters into ordinary lives.
   - *Middle:* Shale dead, Pell untouched; Ruth leaves with Billy, the watch finally set to the right time.
   - *Low Standing:* Ruth becomes what she hunted; the Outfit remembers her as a warning.
Epilogue: spring 1900, playable, with consequences visible in the world.

## Chapter 1 missions (vertical slice)
1. **Rider from the West** — ride the last miles into Bitter Spring along the river road at golden hour; Ruth and her
   horse; first view of the town; stable the horse; a newsboy shouting about the syndicate's land offer.
2. **The Drover** — at Willow Bend, two syndicate toughs are finishing off a wounded drover (Hap). Choice: talk
   (Standing+) or draw; gunfight tutorial with Nerve; patch Hap; establish the camp.
3. **Inquiries** — the Gilded Spur saloon and the sheriff's office: asking after Arliss Doane; a poker table;
   the sheriff warns her off; the grave on the hill (fresh, two days old).
4. **Greer's Post** — Greer sells the Shales' whereabouts; the Shales' men ambush Ruth on the road back; running
   fight on horseback across the river; one survivor talks (or doesn't).
5. **A Fire at Willow Bend** — night; the camp is raided; Hap, Billy (cameo) and the horses; Ruth decides to stay.

## Writing rules
- Every line must do two jobs (character + information, or plot + subtext).
- Period voice without pastiche: no modern idioms ("okay" is fine in 1899; "awesome" is not), no thee/thou, no
  cowboy-movie catchphrases. Specific nouns over adjectives.
- Companions comment on what the player actually did (honour, kills, gifts, missions); barks reflect Standing.
- Strangers get names and wants; nobody is only an obstacle.
